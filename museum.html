OUR STORY

Founded to Preserve
Egypt's Irreplaceable Heritage

The Egyptian Museum in Cairo is the world's oldest and most comprehensive museum dedicated to ancient Egyptian civilization. Established in 1902, it sits at the heart of Cairo's Tahrir Square in a neoclassical building that has itself become an icon of the city.

The museum was conceived after decades of uncontrolled excavation and export of Egyptian artifacts to European collections. Under the leadership of French Egyptologist Auguste Mariette, Egypt established its own Antiquities Service in 1858—the first serious institutional effort to preserve and study the nation's archaeological heritage.

The current building, designed by Marcel Dourgnon following an 1895 international competition, was inaugurated on November 15, 1902, by Khedive Abbas II.

ARCHITECTURE

The Building

Marcel Dourgnon's design blends neoclassical European architecture with Egyptian motifs. The facade features Ionic columns, large arched windows, and decorative reliefs referencing pharaonic art. The central rotunda rises to a glass-and-iron dome, flooding the atrium with natural light.

The building spans approximately 15,000 square meters across two floors, containing 107 halls organized by historical period and artifact type. The ground floor is dedicated to large monuments and statues; the upper floor houses smaller objects, papyri, jewelry, and the Tutankhamun treasures.

THE COLLECTIONS

Major Collections

5,000+ OBJECTS

Tutankhamun's Treasures

The complete funerary assemblage discovered in the Valley of the Kings in 1922 — the most intact royal tomb ever found.

27 ROYAL MUMMIES

Royal Mummies Hall

The mummies of pharaohs and queens, including Ramses II, Seti I, Thutmose III, and Hatshepsut.

12,000+ OBJECTS

Old Kingdom Collection

Masterworks of the pyramid-building age: the Narmer Palette, Rahotep and Nofret statues, Menkaure triads.

800+ OBJECTS

Amarna Collection

Artifacts from the revolutionary reign of Akhenaten, including the famous bust of Nefertiti's family.

40,000+ PAPYRI

Papyrus Collection

The world's largest collection of ancient papyrus documents — religious texts, administrative records, literary works.

10,000+ PIECES

Jewelry & Adornment

Royal and private jewelry spanning 4,000 years — gold, lapis lazuli, faience, carnelian, and turquoise.

MISSION

To Preserve, Study, and Share

The Egyptian Museum's mission is to collect, preserve, and interpret the material heritage of ancient Egypt as well as scholarship, education, and the enrichment of all people.

VISION

A Digital and Physical Legacy

We envision a museum that leads both as a physical institution and a digital resource—managing Egypt's heritage across generations of visitors everywhere.

DEVELOPMENT TIMELINE

Museum History

1835

First Antiquities Service founded by Muhammad Ali Pasha; artifacts moved to the Cairo Citadel.

1858

Auguste Mariette establishes the Egyptian Antiquities Organization and a proper museum at Boulaq.

1891

The Boulaq collection relocated to a palace in Giza after flood damage.

1897

Design competition won by French architect Marcel Dourgnon for a new museum on Tahrir Square.

1902

The new Egyptian Museum inaugurated by Khedive Abbas II on November 15. Gaston Maspero serves as director.

1922

Howard Carter's discovery of Tutankhamun's tomb; treasures transferred to the museum over several years.

1952

Egyptian Revolution; the museum undergoes nationalization and governance transitions.

1983

Major renovation and reorganization of exhibition halls begins.

2011

Some artifacts damaged during unrest; extensive restoration effort initiated.

2021

New Grand Egyptian Museum (GEM) project nears completion; selected collections begin transfer.

2024

Egyptian Museum Cairo continues as a heritage site; new conservation and digital projects launched.

CONSERVATION & RESTORATION

Conservation & Restoration

The museum operates specialized conservation laboratories for stone, metal, organic materials, and textiles. International partnerships with institutions in Europe, Japan, and the United States support ongoing restoration of fragile artifacts. The wooden furniture from Tutankhamun's tomb, for instance, required decades of careful stabilization.

✎
RESEARCH & EDUCATION

Research & Education

The museum collaborates with universities, research institutes, and archaeological missions worldwide. The library holds over 50,000 volumes on Egyptology, archaeology, and related disciplines. Educational programs for schools, students, and public visitors run throughout the year in multiple languages.
