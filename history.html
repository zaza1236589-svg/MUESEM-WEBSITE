Egyptian History

Five Thousand Years

of Civilization

c. 1550–1070 BC

New Kingdom

Egypt's imperial age — the most prosperous and powerful period in pharaonic history. The empire stretched from Nubia to Syria; Thebes became the religious capital; the Valley of the Kings was carved; and Tutankhamun's treasures were created.

Notable Rulers

Ahmose I Thutmose III Hatshepsut Akhenaten Tutankhamun Ramses II Ramses III

Major Events

  1. Ahmose I founds the 18th Dynasty (c. 1550 BC)
  2. Hatshepsut's reign as female pharaoh (c. 1473 BC)
  3. Battle of Kadesh — history's first recorded peace treaty (1259 BC)
  4. Tutankhamun's burial (c. 1323 BC)
  5. Ramses II constructs Abu Simbel (c. 1265 BC)
  6. Valley of the Kings used for 500 years

Cultural Achievements

The great temple complexes of Karnak and Luxor; the Valley of the Kings; Tutankhamun's treasures — the greatest collection in the museum. Egyptian art reached its greatest refinement.

Representative Artifacts in the Museum

  • Tutankhamun's entire funerary assemblage
  • Colossal statue of Ramses II
  • Amarna Collection
  • Book of the Dead papyri

c. 1650–1550 BC

Second Intermediate Period

Egypt's fragmentation under Hyksos rulers from Western Asia, who introduced new military technology including the horse-drawn chariot. The 17th Dynasty Theban rulers eventually expelled the Hyksos and reunited the kingdom.

Notable Rulers

Kamose Seqenenre Tao

Major Events

  1. Hyksos establish rule in Lower Egypt (c. 1650 BC)
  2. Theban 17th Dynasty resists Hyksos rule
  3. Ahmose I expels the Hyksos (c. 1550 BC)
  4. Reunification and founding of the New Kingdom

Cultural Achievements

Egypt adopted the horse-drawn chariot and composite bow from the Hyksos — technologies that would power New Kingdom military expansion. Ahmose I's mummy is in the museum's Royal Mummies Hall.

Representative Artifacts in the Museum

  • Daggers and military objects
  • Scarab seals
  • Royal Mummies of late 17th Dynasty

c. 2055–1650 BC

Middle Kingdom

Egypt's classical age of literature and art. Reunified by the 11th Dynasty and reaching its zenith under the 12th Dynasty, this period saw sophisticated royal portraiture, great literary works, and expanded trade networks.

Notable Rulers

Mentuhotep II Amenemhat I Senusret I Senusret III Amenemhat III

Major Events

  1. Reunification under Mentuhotep II (c. 2055 BC)
  2. Capital moved to Thebes, then Itjtawy
  3. Conquest of Nubia to the Second Cataract
  4. Trade with Punt and the Aegean world

Cultural Achievements

The Golden Age of Egyptian literature: the Story of Sinuhe, the Eloquent Peasant, and the Prophecy of Neferti. The museum's treasure of Lahun jewels dates to this period.

Representative Artifacts in the Museum

  • Wooden models of Meketre
  • Portrait statues of Senusret III

c. 2181–2055 BC

First Intermediate Period

Following the collapse of Old Kingdom central authority, Egypt fragmented into competing regional powers. Despite political instability, art and literature flourished, with regional workshops developing distinct styles.

Notable Rulers

Mentuhotep I Intef I Intef II Intef III

Major Events

  1. Collapse of the Memphite monarchy (c. 2181 BC)
  2. Division between Herakleopolis and Thebes
  3. Development of regional artistic traditions
  4. Reunification by Mentuhotep II (c. 2055 BC)

Cultural Achievements

Regional artistic traditions developed during this politically divided period.

Representative Artifacts in the Museum

  • Wooden models
  • Portrait statues

c. 2686–2181 BC

Old Kingdom

The 'Age of the Pyramids' — ancient Egypt's first golden age. A centralized state mobilized the country's resources to build the most ambitious architectural projects in human history.

Notable Rulers

Djoser Sneferu Khufu Khafre Menkaure Unas

Major Events

  1. Djoser's Step Pyramid built at Saqqara (c. 2667 BC)
  2. Great Pyramid of Giza built (c. 2560 BC)
  3. Sphinx carved (c. 2530 BC)
  4. Pyramid Texts composed (c. 2350 BC)

Cultural Achievements

Monumental pyramid architecture; development of the royal funerary complex; creation of some of the greatest sculpture in Egyptian art history.

Representative Artifacts in the Museum

  • Triad of Menkaure
  • Seated Statue of Djoser
  • Statues of Rahotep and Nofret
  • Mycerinus Triads

c. 3150–2686 BC

Early Dynastic Period

The unification of Upper and Lower Egypt under King Narmer inaugurated the pharaonic tradition. The first two dynasties established the administrative, religious, and artistic foundations of Egyptian civilization.

Notable Rulers

Narmer Hor-Aha Djer Den Qa'a Khasekhemwy

Major Events

  1. Unification of Egypt by Narmer (c. 3150 BC)
  2. Memphis founded as the first capitalt
  3. Hieroglyphic writing fully developed
  4. Royal burial traditions established at Abydos

Cultural Achievements

The Narmer Palette — in the museum's collection — is one of the oldest historical documents in the world, recording the unification of the Two Lands.

Representative Artifacts in the Museum

  • Royal funerary goods from Abydos
  • Early hieroglyphic tablets
  • Narmer Palette

c. 700,000–3150 BC

Prehistoric Egypt

Before the pharaohs, Nile Valley communities developed agriculture, pottery, and the foundations of Egyptian religion over hundreds of thousands of years.

Notable Rulers

Pre-Dynastic chieftains Scorpion I (c. 3200 BC) Scorpion II (c. 3100 BC)

Major Events

  1. First human habitation along the Nile (c. 700,000 BC)
  2. Neolithic farming communities established (c. 5000 BC)
  3. Naqada culture develops pottery and symbols (c. 4000 BC)
  4. Early consolidation of Upper Egypt chiefdoms (c. 3200 BC)

Cultural Achievements

Development of hieroglyphic writing precursors, sophisticated burial practices, and early trade networks across the eastern Mediterranean.

Representative Artifacts in the Museum

  • Gerzean pottery
  • Cosmetic palettes
  • Ivory combs and figurines

c. 1070–664 BC

Third Intermediate Period

Political fragmentation and competition among Libyan, Nubian, and Assyrian powers. Despite political weakness, Egyptian culture remained vital, and the Nubian 25th Dynasty produced some of the finest sculpture of the late pharaonic period.

Notable Rulers

Psusennes I Shoshenq I Taharqa Tantamani

Major Events

  1. Division between Tanis pharaohs and Theban priests
  2. Libyan dynasties rule northern Egypt
  3. Nubian 25th Dynasty unifies Egypt (c. 747 BC)
  4. Assyrian invasion and sacking of Thebes (663 BC)

Cultural Achievements

Royal treasures of Tanis (burial equipment rivaling Tutankhamun in some respects); late period sculpture of exceptional quality.

Representative Artifacts in the Museum

  • Tanis Royal Treasure
  • Silver coffins
  • 25th Dynasty sculpture

664–332 BC

Late Period

Egypt's final centuries of independence — a renaissance of Old Kingdom artistic models, intense religious activity, and repeated foreign invasions by Assyrians, Persians, and finally Alexander the Great.

Notable Rulers

Apries Apries I Ahmose II I Nectanebo I Nectanebo II

Major Events

  1. 26th Saite Dynasty restores pharaonic tradition (664 BC)
  2. First Persian occupation (525–404 BC)
  3. Egypt's last native pharaoh, Nectanebo II (360–342 BC)
  4. Second Persian occupation
  5. Alexander the Great's conquest (332 BC)

Cultural Achievements

A deliberate revival of Old Kingdom artistic and religious models. The 'archaizing' style produced some of the finest bronze statuary of the ancient world.

Representative Artifacts in the Museum

  • Late Period bronzes
  • Animal mummies and cult objects
  • Naos shrines

332–30 BC

Ptolemaic Period

Following Alexander's conquest, his general Ptolemy I founded the last royal dynasty of ancient Egypt. The Ptolemaic period blended Greek and Egyptian culture, producing a uniquely syncretic civilization — and ending with Cleopatra VII and the Roman conquest.

Notable Rulers

Ptolemy I Ptolemy III Cleopatra VII

Major Events

  1. Alexander the Great conquers Egypt (332 BC)
  2. Ptolemy I Soter founds the dynasty (305 BC)
  3. Rosetta Stone inscribed (196 BC)
  4. Cleopatra VII's reign (51–30 BC)
  5. Roman conquest; Egypt becomes a province (30 BC)

Cultural Achievements

The Rosetta Stone (now in the British Museum); the great temples of Edfu, Dendara, and Philae; the Library of Alexandria; syncretic Greco-Egyptian art forms.

Representative Artifacts in the Museum

  • Ptolemaic reliefs
  • Demotic and bilingual papyri
  • Greco-Egyptian sculpture

30 BC–395 ADC

Roman Period

Egypt as a Roman province — the empire's breadbasket and most prized possession. Egyptian religious traditions continued under Roman administration, producing unique Fayum mummy portraits and late syncretic religious art before the rise of Christianity ended the pharaonic world.

Notable Rulers

Augustus Caesar (as pharaoh) I Emperor as pharaoh tradition continues

Major Events

  1. Emperor as pharaoh tradition continues
  2. Cleopatra's death ends Ptolemaic dynasty (30 BC)
  3. Egypt becomes a Roman province
  4. Fayum mummy portrait tradition (1st–3rd c. AD)
  5. Christianity spreads through Egypt

Cultural Achievements

Fayum mummy portraits — naturalistic painted panel portraits on linen mummy wrappings, the direct ancestors of Byzantine icon painting.

Representative Artifacts in the Museum

  • Fayum mummy portraits
  • Roman-era mummies
  • Coptic textiles and objects
